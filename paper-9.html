Incident Report # MON-2024/0312

At 21.30 hours on 04 March 2024, the Goma Traffic Police Department received an emergency call reporting a severe traffic collision on Boulevard Kanyamuhanga near the Virunga Hotel. Patrol Unit 'Bravo' arrived at the scene at 21.42 hours and found an unconscious female pedestrian lying on the edge of the road beside a damaged bicycle. Witnesses stated that a white Toyota Hilux double-cabin pickup truck with black UN markings had struck the woman while speeding toward the city center and failed to stop. Paramedics arrived at 21.55 hours and transported the victim to Heal Africa Hospital, where she was pronounced dead at 22.20 hours due to massive internal bleeding. She was identified through her voter card as Esperance Kavira, a 27-year-old primary school teacher.

Follow-Up Report # MON-2024/0312/01

Reference is made to the fatal hit-and-run reported on 04 March 2024. At 22.30 hours, UNPOL traffic investigators joined local police to examine the accident scene. Officers collected fragments of a broken right-side headlight lens and a detached black plastic side mirror bearing a serial barcode. Skid marks measuring 18 meters were documented on the wet asphalt. Police interviewed a night security guard from the Virunga Hotel, Patrick Bahati, 41 years old, who stated that the white pickup truck had partial registration numbers ending in '409' and was carrying a blue plastic water tank in the rear bed.

Follow-Up Report # MON-2024/0312/02

Reference is made to the fatal hit-and-run reported on 04 March 2024. On 05 March at 08.00 hours, UNPOL investigators checked the UN Mission Transport Fleet database in Goma for all Toyota Hilux vehicles carrying water tanks or assigned to logistics units. Out of 42 vehicles checked, only one matched the partial plate '409': vehicle registration UN-4409, assigned to the Water and Sanitation Engineering Unit at Lava Site Camp. At 09.15 hours, investigators visited Lava Site Camp and found vehicle UN-4409 parked inside a maintenance hangar with a shattered right headlight and a missing right-side mirror.

Follow-Up Report # MON-2024/0312/03

Reference is made to the fatal hit-and-run reported on 04 March 2024. Investigators checked the camp gate logbook and GPS fleet tracker for vehicle UN-4409. The logbook showed that the vehicle had been signed out at 19.00 hours on 04 March by a national staff driver named Celestin Mugisha, 35 years old, for an authorized fuel run, and returned to the camp at 22.05 hours. However, GPS records indicated that after refueling, the truck remained stationary outside 'La Terrasse' bar in Himbi district from 20.00 hours until 21.20 hours before speeding down Boulevard Kanyamuhanga.

Follow-Up Report # MON-2024/0312/04

Reference is made to the fatal hit-and-run reported on 04 March 2024. At 11.00 hours on 05 March, detectives visited 'La Terrasse' bar in Himbi and interviewed the bartender, Solange Furaha, 29 years old. She confirmed that Celestin Mugisha was a regular customer and had drunk four bottles of Primus beer between 20.00 and 21.15 hours the previous evening. According to Furaha, Mugisha received an urgent phone call from his wife saying their son had a high fever, so he rushed out to his UN truck in an intoxicated state and drove away rapidly.

SECOND PART OF CLA – NARRATIVE

Reference is made to the fatal hit-and-run involving UN vehicle UN-4409 on 04 March 2024. On 05 March at 13.30 hours, UNPOL Special Investigation Unit officers and Congolese National Police detectives went to Celestin Mugisha’s residence in Katindo neighborhood to arrest him. Mugisha was not at home, but his wife, Nadine Mugisha, 32 years old, informed officers that he had packed a small suitcase and taken a motorcycle taxi to the bus terminal to travel to Bukavu. Police immediately alerted all roadblocks and dispatched a patrol unit to the Goma Central Bus Terminal.

At 14.15 hours, officers located Mugisha sitting inside a passenger bus scheduled to depart for Bukavu at 14.30 hours. He was arrested without incident and taken to the Goma Traffic Police Headquarters. A breathalyzer test conducted at 14.45 hours still detected residual alcohol of 0.04 percent in his system. During formal questioning, Mugisha broke down in tears and admitted that he was driving while intoxicated, panicked after hitting the cyclist, and hid the damaged truck inside the camp hangar hoping no one would notice before morning.

DIALOGUE

Chief Investigator: Good afternoon, Lieutenant. Have you apprehended the driver of vehicle UN-4409?

Traffic Lieutenant: Yes, Sir. We arrested Celestin Mugisha at 14.15 hours today at the Goma Central Bus Terminal.

Chief Investigator: Was he trying to flee the city?

Traffic Lieutenant: Correct. His wife told us he packed a suitcase and went to catch a bus to Bukavu. We found him sitting inside a bus that was about to leave at 14.30 hours.

Chief Investigator: Did you conduct an alcohol test upon his arrest?

Traffic Lieutenant: We did at 14.45 hours, and even after 17 hours, the breathalyzer still showed 0.04 percent alcohol.

Chief Investigator: Did he confess to the hit-and-run?

Traffic Lieutenant: Yes, he confessed completely. He admitted he drank four beers at La Terrasse bar, panicked when he hit the woman on Boulevard Kanyamuhanga, and hid the truck in the maintenance hangar at Lava Site Camp.

Chief Investigator: Have you notified the UN Mission Legal Office regarding immunity waiver procedures?

Traffic Lieutenant: Yes, the UNPOL Chief has already submitted the preliminary report so local prosecutors can proceed with charges of vehicular manslaughter.
