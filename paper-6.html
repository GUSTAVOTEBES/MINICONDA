PART 1: READING COMPREHENSION #1000

INCIDENT REPORT # UN-JUB/2024/0892

At 07.45 hours on 14 October 2024, the Malakal Central Police Station received an emergency call from Samuel Deng, a 48-year-old security guard working at the "Nile Logistics" humanitarian warehouse located on Airport Road. He reported that upon arriving for his morning shift at 07.30 hours, he discovered that the main padlock of the storage facility had been cut and the metal door was partially open. A Police Patrol Unit arrived at the scene at 08.05 hours and secured the perimeter. Inside the warehouse, officers observed scattered cardboard boxes and tire tracks leading out of the loading bay. The warehouse manager, Ms. Grace Ajak, arrived at 08.30 hours and conducted a rapid inventory. She confirmed that 45 boxes of high-energy biscuits and 12 solar-powered water pumps intended for an internally displaced persons (IDP) camp were missing. No security cameras were installed inside the building, and the night guard was nowhere to be found.

FOLLOW-UP REPORT # UN-JUB/2024/0892/01

Reference is made to the burglary reported on 14 October 2024. At 10.00 hours, detectives interviewed the missing night guard, Peter Gatluak, 34 years old, at his residence in Hai Jalaba district after he failed to answer his mobile phone. Gatluak appeared nervous and had a fresh bruise on his left cheek. Initially, he claimed he had fallen asleep in the guardroom and heard nothing. However, when questioned about his facial injury, he admitted that around 02.15 hours, three armed men wearing dark civilian clothes and ski masks broke into the compound. They struck him with the butt of a rifle, tied his hands with plastic zip-ties, and locked him inside an empty shipping container behind the warehouse. Gatluak stated that he managed to free himself around 06.00 hours and ran home in panic without reporting the incident to the police.

FOLLOW-UP REPORT # UN-JUB/2024/0892/02

Reference is made to the burglary reported on 14 October 2024. On 15 October at 09.15 hours, police canvassed local businesses along Airport Road. They interviewed Ibrahim Musa, 52 years old, owner of a small fuel station located 300 meters north of the Nile Logistics warehouse. Musa stated that at approximately 02.45 hours on 14 October, a white Mitsubishi pickup truck with a damaged rear bumper stopped at his station to buy 40 liters of diesel. He noticed that the truck bed was covered with a blue tarpaulin, though several boxes marked with humanitarian aid logos were visible underneath. Musa recognized the driver as a local mechanic named David Lual, who frequently repaired vehicles in the area. According to Musa, there were two other men sitting inside the cabin, and the vehicle headed south toward the river port after refueling.

FOLLOW-UP REPORT # UN-JUB/2024/0892/03

Reference is made to the burglary reported on 14 October 2024. On 15 October at 14.00 hours, acting on the witness statement, a joint patrol of local police and UN Police (UNPOL) advisors visited David Lual’s auto repair workshop in the Southern Industrial Zone. Upon arrival, officers found the white Mitsubishi pickup truck parked behind the garage with the damaged rear bumper matching the witness description. A search of the workshop revealed 30 boxes of high-energy biscuits and all 12 stolen solar water pumps hidden under old tires. David Lual, 39 years old, was arrested at the scene. During initial questioning, Lual denied stealing the goods, claiming that a man named "Kobra" paid him 200 US dollars at 01.30 hours to transport the cargo from the warehouse to his garage and hold it until the next evening.

FOLLOW-UP REPORT # UN-JUB/2024/0892/04

Reference is made to the burglary reported on 14 October 2024. On 16 October at 11.00 hours, investigators cross-checked the call logs from the night guard Peter Gatluak’s mobile phone, which had been seized as evidence. The records revealed three outgoing phone calls to David Lual’s number between 00.30 hours and 01.15 hours on the night of the robbery. Confronted with this evidence, Peter Gatluak broke down and confessed that he had conspired with David Lual and two other accomplices to stage the robbery because he had not received his salary for four months. He admitted that he had unlocked the main gate for them and asked Lual to hit him lightly in the face to make the assault look realistic. The remaining 15 boxes of biscuits had already been sold at the Central Market early on 14 October.

PART 2: LISTENING / REPORT WRITING

NARRATIVE

Reference is made to the burglary at the Nile Logistics humanitarian warehouse on 14 October 2024. Following the arrest of the mechanic David Lual and the confession of the night guard Peter Gatluak on 16 October, the Malakal Police Station Commander authorized an immediate operation to apprehend the remaining two suspects and recover the missing food supplies. During his interrogation, Gatluak identified the two accomplices as brothers: Thomas Bol, 28 years old, and Simon Bol, 31 years old, both unemployed residents of the Munuki neighborhood. Gatluak explained that Thomas Bol had arranged the sale of the 15 missing boxes of high-energy biscuits to a local trader at the Central Market for 450 US dollars.

At 15.30 hours on 16 October, a police tactical team surrounded a mud-brick house owned by the Bol brothers' uncle in Munuki. Officers knocked on the front door and announced their presence. Simon Bol attempted to flee through a back window carrying a black backpack, but he was quickly intercepted and arrested by officers positioned in the rear alley. Inside the backpack, police discovered 300 US dollars in cash and a pair of heavy-duty bolt cutters used to break the warehouse padlock. Thomas Bol was found hiding under a bed inside the house and surrendered without resistance. Both suspects were transported to Central Police Station, where they admitted their involvement. Later that afternoon, police visited stall number 14 at the Central Market, owned by trader Mary Nyandeng, who voluntarily handed over the remaining 15 boxes of biscuits, stating she did not know they were stolen humanitarian aid.

DIALOGUE

UNPOL Advisor: Good afternoon, Inspector. Thank you for meeting with me. Could you brief me on the latest developments regarding the Nile Logistics warehouse theft?

Police Inspector: Good afternoon. Yes, we have made significant progress today, 16 October. After analyzing the phone records, we confirmed that the night guard, Peter Gatluak, was in direct contact with the mechanic, David Lual, right before the incident.

UNPOL Advisor: So the night guard was an insider?

Police Inspector: Exactly. Gatluak confessed this morning. He told us he hadn't been paid his salary for four months, so he planned the theft with Lual and two brothers named Thomas and Simon Bol. Gatluak unlocked the gate and even asked Lual to hit him on the cheek to fake an attack.

UNPOL Advisor: Have you located the Bol brothers yet?

Police Inspector: Yes, we arrested both of them at 15.30 hours today at their uncle's house in the Munuki neighborhood. Simon Bol tried to escape through a back window with a black backpack, while Thomas was found hiding under a bed.

UNPOL Advisor: Did you find any evidence on them?

Police Inspector: Inside Simon's backpack, we recovered 300 US dollars and the bolt cutters they used to cut the warehouse padlock. They admitted they had sold the remaining 15 boxes of biscuits at the Central Market for 450 US dollars.

UNPOL Advisor: Were you able to recover those 15 boxes as well?

Police Inspector: Yes. We went to stall number 14 at the Central Market. The shop owner, Mary Nyandeng, cooperated fully and returned all 15 boxes. She claimed she had no idea the biscuits were stolen humanitarian supplies.

UNPOL Advisor: That is excellent police work, Inspector. All four suspects are in custody and all stolen aid has been recovered. Please ensure the final case file is sent to the prosecutor tomorrow morning.
