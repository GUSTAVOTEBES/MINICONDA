Incident Report # MLI-2022/0419

At 16.10 hours on 18 May 2022, the Gao Regional Police Command received a radio distress call from an international NGO convoy traveling along National Highway 17, approximately 25 kilometers south of Gao. According to the convoy leader, Dr. Amadou Traore, 47 years old, their two white Nissan Patrol vehicles were ambushed at 15.40 hours by four armed men riding on two motorcycles. The assailants blocked the road with large rocks, fired several shots into the air, and forced the staff out of their vehicles. The attackers stole three laptop computers, two satellite phones, a medical emergency kit, and 3,500 Euros in cash intended for local clinic workers. One driver, Boubacar Diallo, 39 years old, suffered a deep laceration on his forehead after being struck with a pistol.

Follow-Up Report # MLI-2022/0419/01

Reference is made to the convoy robbery reported on 18 May 2022. At 17.00 hours, a joint patrol of Malian National Police and UNPOL officers arrived at the ambush site and secured the area. Officers recovered five spent 9mm shell casings on the asphalt and observed fresh motorcycle tire tracks heading east toward the desert village of Djebock. An ambulance transported the injured driver, Boubacar Diallo, to Gao Regional Hospital, where he received eight stitches and was kept overnight for observation. The remaining four NGO staff members were unharmed and escorted back to the UN supercamp in Gao.

Follow-Up Report # MLI-2022/0419/02

Reference is made to the convoy robbery reported on 18 May 2022. On 19 May at 08.30 hours, investigators interviewed a shepherd named Oumar Dicko, 60 years old, who was tending his goats 500 meters from the highway during the attack. Dicko stated that he saw two black Yamaha motorcycles parked behind a sand dune about 30 minutes before the convoy arrived. He noticed that one of the riders was wearing a bright green turban and had a distinctive scar on his right forearm. Dicko also overheard the men arguing in the local Tamasheq dialect about waiting for 'the white cars from the hospital'.

Follow-Up Report # MLI-2022/0419/03

Reference is made to the convoy robbery reported on 18 May 2022. At 11.45 hours on 19 May, police contacted the telecommunications provider to trace the two stolen satellite phones. Technical data revealed that one of the phones had been switched on briefly at 21.15 hours on 18 May in the Chateau district of Gao, near a well-known electronics repair shop owned by Hamidou Maiga. Detectives visited the shop at 13.00 hours and questioned Maiga, 33 years old. Maiga admitted that a young man wearing a green turban had brought him two satellite phones and three laptops the previous night, asking him to erase all data and unlock the passwords.

Follow-Up Report # MLI-2022/0419/04

Reference is made to the convoy robbery reported on 18 May 2022. Hamidou Maiga told detectives that he refused to unlock the devices because he suspected they were stolen, so the man left the shop angrily on a black Yamaha motorcycle without a rear mudguard. Maiga recognized the man as Issa Ag Mohamed, 29 years old, a former security guard who had been dismissed from the same NGO three months earlier for stealing fuel. Police obtained Issa’s file from the NGO human resources office, which confirmed his home address in the Fourth Quarter of Gao.

SECOND PART OF CLA – NARRATIVE

Reference is made to the robbery of the humanitarian convoy on 18 May 2022. Based on the identification provided by the electronics shop owner, police placed Issa Ag Mohamed’s residence in the Fourth Quarter of Gao under surveillance on the evening of 19 May. At 20.15 hours, detectives observed Issa entering the courtyard on a black Yamaha motorcycle missing its rear mudguard. Ten minutes later, another man arrived on foot carrying a brown leather bag. Joint police units immediately raided the compound and arrested both individuals without firing a shot.

The second suspect was identified as Aliou Touré, 31 years old, who had a prominent scar on his right forearm matching the shepherd's description. Inside the brown leather bag, officers recovered all three stolen laptops, both satellite phones, the medical kit, and 2,900 Euros in cash. During interrogation, Issa confessed that he planned the ambush out of revenge after being fired by the NGO, and admitted he had already spent 600 Euros to buy food and motorcycle parts. Both men were remanded in custody awaiting trial.

DIALOGUE

Police Commander: Welcome, Inspector. Please brief me on the arrests made last night in the Fourth Quarter.

Detective Inspector: Yes, Sir. At 20.15 hours on 19 May, our surveillance team saw Issa Ag Mohamed arrive at his house on a black Yamaha motorcycle without a rear mudguard.

Police Commander: Was he alone at that time?

Detective Inspector: Initially yes, but ten minutes later a second man walked into the courtyard carrying a brown leather bag. We raided the compound immediately and arrested both of them.

Police Commander: Who was the second man?

Detective Inspector: His name is Aliou Touré, 31 years old. He has a long scar on his right forearm, just as the shepherd described.

Police Commander: Did you recover the stolen NGO property?

Detective Inspector: We found the three laptops, two satellite phones, and the medical kit inside the brown bag. However, only 2,900 Euros remained out of the 3,500 Euros stolen.

Police Commander: What happened to the missing 600 Euros?

Detective Inspector: Issa confessed that he spent 600 Euros on food and motorcycle parts. He also admitted he organized the ambush to take revenge on the NGO for firing him.
