Incident Report # MIN-2023/1104

At 14.20 hours on 12 November 2023, the Joint Border Control Post in Garoua-Boulai received a tip-off from a local customs clerk regarding a suspicious commercial cargo truck heading toward Bangui. A joint patrol of national gendarmerie and UN Police (UNPOL) officers intercepted a red Volvo cargo truck, registration plate CA-8821-B, at Checkpoint Alpha at 14.50 hours. The driver, a 44-year-old male named Jean-Pierre Mokoko, presented manifest documents declaring a shipment of 150 bags of cement. During a physical inspection of the trailer, officers noticed that several cement bags near the cabin had been re-stitched. Upon cutting open three bags, police discovered 18 AK-47 assault rifles and 12 wooden crates of 7.62mm ammunition hidden inside waterproof plastic wrap. The truck was impounded, and the driver was taken into custody.

Follow-Up Report # MIN-2023/1104/01

Reference is made to the weapons seizure reported on 12 November 2023. At 17.00 hours, investigators interviewed the driver, Jean-Pierre Mokoko, at the Bouar Gendarmerie Brigade. Mokoko stated that he worked for 'Trans-Oubangui Freight', a transport company based in Bangui. He claimed that he had picked up the cement shipment from a warehouse in Douala three days earlier, but made an unscheduled stop at a roadside garage in Bertoua on the night of 11 November. According to Mokoko, two armed men approached him at the garage and offered him 500,000 CFA francs to transport six sealed pallets to a farm outside Bossembele. He insisted he did not know the pallets contained weapons and stated he only agreed because his youngest daughter needed urgent surgery.

Follow-Up Report # MIN-2023/1104/02

Reference is made to the weapons seizure reported on 12 November 2023. On 13 November at 09.30 hours, detectives contacted the owner of 'Trans-Oubangui Freight', Ms. Clarisse Ndombe, 51 years old, at her office in Bangui. She confirmed that Mokoko had been employed as a driver for six years without any prior disciplinary record. However, Ms. Ndombe checked the company’s GPS tracking logs and revealed that the truck had deviated 14 kilometers off the main highway near Baboua for nearly three hours on the morning of 12 November. She also informed investigators that Mokoko had recently purchased a new plot of land in Bimbo district, contradicting his claim of financial hardship.

Follow-Up Report # MIN-2023/1104/03

Reference is made to the weapons seizure reported on 12 November 2023. At 13.15 hours on 13 November, police examined the seized ammunition crates and found military batch markings belonging to a regional militia group operating near the northern border. Forensic technicians lifted three clear fingerprints from the plastic wrapping around the AK-47 rifles. When cross-referenced in the national database, one fingerprint matched a known arms broker named Ibrahim Ousmane, 38 years old, who had escaped from Ngaragba Central Prison in 2021.

Follow-Up Report # MIN-2023/1104/04

Reference is made to the weapons seizure reported on 12 November 2023. At 16.00 hours on 13 November, UNPOL advisors assisted local detectives in analyzing Mokoko’s mobile phone. Text messages retrieved from an encrypted application showed that Mokoko had communicated directly with Ibrahim Ousmane five times over the past two weeks. One message from Ousmane instructed Mokoko to deliver the 'hardware' to a yellow warehouse behind the old cotton factory in Bossembele before 18.00 hours on 12 November.

SECOND PART OF CLA – NARRATIVE

Reference is made to the illicit weapons smuggling case reported on 12 November 2023. Following the analysis of GPS logs and mobile phone records on 13 November, a joint task force comprising the National Gendarmerie and UNPOL special investigators organized a covert operation in Bossembele. At 05.30 hours on 14 November 2023, tactical units surrounded the yellow warehouse behind the old cotton factory. Officers observed a white Toyota Land Cruiser without license plates parked beside the loading dock. When officers announced their presence and breached the metal gate, two men attempted to flee on foot toward a nearby forest.

Police fired two warning shots into the air, forcing both suspects to surrender. The first suspect was identified as the fugitive arms broker, Ibrahim Ousmane, 38 years old, and the second was his younger cousin, Moussa Ousmane, 26 years old. A thorough search of the warehouse uncovered 250,000 US dollars in counterfeit banknotes, a satellite phone, and detailed maps marking UN peacekeeping patrol routes in the western sector. Both suspects were arrested and transferred to Bangui under heavy escort, while the prosecutor filed formal charges of arms trafficking and conspiracy against state security.

DIALOGUE

UNPOL Advisor: Good morning, Captain. Could you update me on the operation conducted in Bossembele earlier today?

Gendarmerie Captain: Good morning. Yes, at 05.30 hours on 14 November, our tactical team surrounded the yellow warehouse behind the old cotton factory.

UNPOL Advisor: Did you encounter any resistance when entering the compound?

Gendarmerie Captain: No armed resistance, but two men tried to run toward the forest behind the building. We fired two warning shots into the air and they immediately stopped.

UNPOL Advisor: Were you able to confirm their identities?

Gendarmerie Captain: Yes. One of them is Ibrahim Ousmane, the 38-year-old arms broker who escaped from Ngaragba Prison in 2021. The other is his 26-year-old cousin, Moussa Ousmane.

UNPOL Advisor: What did you find inside the warehouse or their vehicle?

Gendarmerie Captain: There was a white Toyota Land Cruiser without license plates outside. Inside the warehouse, we found 250,000 US dollars in counterfeit money, a satellite phone, and maps showing UN patrol routes.

UNPOL Advisor: What is the next step for the suspects?

Gendarmerie Captain: They have been transferred to Bangui maximum security prison, and the public prosecutor has charged them with arms trafficking and conspiracy.
